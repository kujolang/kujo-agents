# Handoff Rules

## Purpose

Prepare a grounded handoff for another agent, model, human collaborator, or automation harness so it can work from accurate project context without rediscovering the inspected sources.

The handoff is not the downstream deliverable. It is the factual base, constraint set, vocabulary, source map, uncertainty map, and "do not say" list.

## Required Handoff Contents

Include:

- One-sentence source-grounded project description.
- Confirmed project facts.
- Inferred relationships, clearly labeled.
- Planned or future ideas, clearly labeled.
- Unknowns and open questions.
- Repository, document, and source map.
- Component, interface, tool, workflow, or domain glossary.
- Audience, user, stakeholder, or use-case evidence.
- Public positioning or claim evidence when present.
- Claims to avoid.
- Execution constraints for the downstream task.

## What To Give Downstream Work

Give the next agent or collaborator enough to understand:

- What the project is.
- What problems sources say it addresses.
- Which components, documents, tools, workflows, or interfaces exist.
- Which terms are real project vocabulary.
- Which sources support which claims.
- Which claims are off-limits.
- Which facts can be used confidently in downstream work.

## What Not To Give Downstream Work

Do not provide:

- Invented project claims.
- Unsupported competitive positioning.
- Finished implementation plans, campaign copy, visual directions, research conclusions, or operational decisions unless separately requested.
- Claims that collapse planned work into current behavior.
- Recommendations that depend on facts not established by the inspected sources.

## Evidence Boundaries

For every downstream-use fact, state whether it is:

- Confirmed.
- Inferred.
- Planned.
- Unknown.

If a fact is unknown, the downstream worker may not present it as true. It may ask for clarification, inspect more sources, or omit it.

## Handoff Tone

Use neutral, technical language.

Avoid:

- Hype.
- Slogans.
- Superlatives.
- Claims of market leadership.
- Emotional positioning not present in the sources.
- Overconfident architectural or product conclusions not present in the sources.

## Final Preflight

Before delivering a handoff:

- Check that every material fact has evidence.
- Check that unsupported claims are in "claims to avoid" or "unknowns".
- Check that planned work is not written as shipped.
- Check that the handoff does not become the downstream deliverable.
- Check that constraints are explicit enough for another model, agent, human collaborator, or automation harness to use safely.
